# Document type: record

A record answers *what actually happened*. It is frozen: written once, about a
bounded scope, and thereafter left alone so a later reader can see what was
believed at the time.

Guidance:

- State the scope in the opening: what was examined, and what was not.
- Report observations, not conclusions dressed as observations. Where you
  inferred something, say that you inferred it.
- Quantify with denominators and say how each number was obtained. A measurement
  with no method is an opinion with digits.
- Where an earlier claim in the record turned out wrong, annotate it — do not
  retell the record. The mistake is part of what happened, and a reader needs to
  see both the belief and the correction.
- Where the mistake is the subject matter, quote it plainly, always framed as the
  mistake, never left readable as a live claim.

## Not its job

Staying current. A record does not track the present; a register does that. When
a record is fully superseded it is retired with a note saying what replaced it
and what must not be repeated from it — an archived document with no such note is
a trap rather than an archive.

## A note on region authority

A record is frozen, so its prose is not a thing a model should be re-authoring.
If you are being asked to author one, the document's managed regions are probably
declared with the wrong authority: a record's prose belongs in a `human` region,
and only a region whose body is genuinely derived from a machine source should be
generated. Author what you are given, and keep it to what the surface supports.
