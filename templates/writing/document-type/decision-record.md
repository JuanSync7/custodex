# Document type: decision record

A decision record answers one question: *what was decided, and does it still
bind?* It captures one decision at one moment, and it is read later by someone
deciding whether it still applies.

Guidance:

- Put the decision in the first lines. A reader who stops after the opening must
  know what was chosen.
- State the context that forced a choice, then the alternatives considered and
  why each was rejected. An alternative with no stated reason reads as one
  nobody thought of.
- Give reasons that are each sufficient alone where you can. A decision resting
  on one reason falls when that reason does.
- Name what this supersedes, and what supersedes it if anything does.
- State the conditions that would reverse it. A decision with no reversal
  condition cannot be revisited honestly — only overturned.
- Record the status plainly: proposed, accepted, superseded.

## Not its job

Being a living catalogue. A decision record is a snapshot: when the decision
changes, it is superseded by a new record rather than edited, so the reasoning
that was actually used remains legible. Cross-references may be maintained; the
decision text is not rewritten.
